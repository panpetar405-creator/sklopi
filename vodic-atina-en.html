Athens — When to Go, Airport, Must-See Sights

When to Visit Athens — the Best Time to See the Acropolis Without the Crowds

Athens is most pleasant to explore in spring (April–May) and early autumn (September–October) — temperatures are mild, while in summer (July–August) the city can hit well over 35°C, which makes long walks around the Acropolis and other open-air sites tough going. Winter is mild and rainy, fine for a shorter city break without the beach.

Athens Airport and Getting to the City Centre

Eleftherios Venizelos Airport (ATH) is Athens' only major airport, about 30–35 km from the centre. You can reach downtown by metro (Blue Line/3, straight to Syntagma in about 40 minutes), by express bus (cheaper, slower, handy at night when the metro runs less often), or by taxi (a fixed city fare, useful in a group, with lots of luggage, or late at night).

How Much a Trip to Athens Costs — Ballpark Prices

  • Acropolis ticket — about €20 in summer (April–October) and about €10 in winter; a combined ticket covering the Acropolis and 6 more archaeological sites costs around €30 and pays off if you're planning more than two sites.
  • Metro from the airport — about €9–11 per person one way; the express bus is cheaper, usually around €5–6.
  • A meal at a taverna in Plaka — from €12–18 per person for a main course, while places outside the tourist zone (e.g. around Monastiraki, off the main street) are usually 20–30% cheaper for similar quality.
  • Coffee/frappé — €3.5–5 in the centre, cheaper in neighbourhoods off the tourist trail (Pangrati, Koukaki).

Must-See Sights in Athens in 3 Days

  • The Acropolis and the Acropolis Museum — go early in the morning or an hour or two before closing; in summer, crowds and heat are the real problem, not the ticket price.
  • Plaka — the old quarter beneath the Acropolis, a good base for walking and eating, within walking distance of most other sights.
  • Monastiraki and the flea market — liveliest on weekend mornings.
  • The National Archaeological Museum — if you're only planning one museum besides the Acropolis, make it this one.
  • Lycabettus Hill — the best view over the city and sunset, especially worthwhile if you're not making it to the islands.

If you have more than 3 days, a ferry to a nearby island (e.g. Aegina, a day trip from the port of Piraeus) breaks up an otherwise purely city-based visit.

Where to Eat in Athens — Local Food to Try

For a real taste of the city, look for small traditional tavernas, not restaurants with a menu in five languages out front — that's almost always a sign of tourist pricing without better quality. Be sure to try: souvlaki (often best from street stalls, not sit-down restaurants), gemista (peppers and tomatoes stuffed with rice), moussaka, and local tzatziki with freshly baked bread. The Psyrri district is a cheaper, more authentic dinner choice than Plaka itself, about a 10-minute walk away.

Hidden Spots in Athens Off the Main Tourist Trail

  • Anafiotika — a tiny Cycladic-style quarter tucked right below the Acropolis, with white houses and narrow stairways; most tourists walk right past the entrance without noticing it.
  • The Exarchia district — an alternative, artsy neighbourhood with street art and local cafés, off the usual tourist route.
  • The National Garden — shade and quiet right behind Parliament, a good break between sightseeing in the heat.
  • The Filopappou Hill viewpoint — an alternative to Lycabettus, fewer crowds, and the view of the Acropolis is actually better than from Lycabettus itself.

Practical Tips Before You Book

  • Documents — Greece is in the Schengen Area; entry requirements (ID card/passport, length of stay) depend on your nationality, so check them before you travel.
  • eSIM — handy for navigating Plaka's narrow streets and for offline maps if the signal gets weak in the metro.
  • Insurance — basic travel insurance included with a booking often has a high excess; it's worth arranging separately if your plans include renting a moped or scooter, a common choice in Greece.
